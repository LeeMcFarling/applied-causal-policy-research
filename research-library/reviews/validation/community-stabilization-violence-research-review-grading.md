## Methodology Note

This grading separates evaluation into three independent artifacts — the source literature, the research review, and the policy brief — to avoid conflating "good evidence" with "good policy." That separation is identified as one of the strongest features of the review process.

## 1. Literature Grade: 17A (A)

The paper is unusually valuable because it does not simply argue that environmental interventions work — it explains how cities operationalize them.

**Strengths:**
- Clearly defined empirical problem (crime concentration)
- Practical implementation focus with explicit scope limitations
- Acknowledgement of uncertainty; discussion of failure modes
- Operational case study (Dallas 2024–2025)
- Randomized evidence supporting major interventions (Branas et al., Chalfin et al.)
- Actionable deployment model with personnel and timeline specifications
- Executive summary appropriately distinguishes environmental interventions from policing from long-term social programs

**Limitation (scope, not rigor):** The authors openly acknowledge they do not attempt to answer poverty, housing, healthcare, labor markets, or long-term social cohesion. This is a limitation of scope rather than rigor.

## 2. Research Review Grade (A-)

**Overall assessment:** High-quality literature receipt with good source discipline throughout.

### Aligned Findings — Excellent
The review distinguishes actual support from extrapolation. It correctly identifies what 17A validates (geographic targeting, environmental interventions, coordination architecture, maintenance, concentrated deployment) rather than claiming the paper validates the entirety of platform. Source discipline is strong.

### Gaps — Strong
The review correctly identifies silences rather than disagreements: third spaces, structural determinants, anti-displacement, federal implementation, long-term social capital. These are exactly the distinctions the template asks reviewers to make.

### Divergences — Strongest Section
The source position → platform position → justification structure matches the template's philosophy almost perfectly. Disagreements are not dismissed; they are explained and documented with rationale.

### Open Questions — Very Good
Questions are genuinely unresolved rather than rhetorical. Tipping-point vs. chronic hotspots, market effects, intervention scale — these are appropriate pilot-design questions.

### Deductions (-):

**1. Language precision — "validates platform" vs. "consistent with"**
The review occasionally moves from "the paper is consistent with [X]" to "this validates platform." These are not identical claims. A stricter formulation would be "provides evidence consistent with" or "supports this mechanism." Recommend revising the Aligned Findings section to use more precise language.

**2. Missing alternative interpretations**
The review should include at least one explicit alternative interpretation of the source findings. For example: Dallas may have succeeded in part because of unusually capable management rather than the intervention architecture itself. The review should acknowledge competing explanations even if it ultimately rejects them — this is what makes the Divergences section adversarially credible.

**3. Generosity toward platform**
An external reviewer should feel the grading process is trying to *disprove* the platform rather than confirm it. The current review is balanced but not adversarial. An additional pass with explicit contrary framing would strengthen the receipt.

## 3. Community Stabilization Brief Grade (A-)

**Overall assessment:** The brief clearly evolved after reading the literature. Additions are substantive rather than cosmetic.

**Documented improvements:**
- Adaptive weekly targeting (from static to recalculated)
- Emerging-deterioration prioritization over chronic hotspot targeting
- CRC operational cadence specified as design requirement
- Explicit maintenance model
- 90-day deployment expectation with validation

**Major strength:** The brief now distinguishes short-term operational stabilization from long-term structural reform. The environmental layer is no longer presented as replacing housing or healthcare — it explicitly connects to downstream stabilization systems. The integration between Community Stabilization → Homelessness Prevention → Healthcare Housing → Workforce Stabilization is becoming increasingly coherent: each document owns a different part of the pipeline rather than duplicating functions.

### Remaining Weaknesses:

**1. Third spaces — evidence overstated**
The brief presents third spaces with nearly the same certainty as lighting interventions. The evidence base for staffed programming is promising but considerably more heterogeneous. Certainty should be softened.

**2. Federal role — unvalidated assumption**
The brief assumes federal coordination improves implementation. 17A neither supports nor rejects this proposition. The claim still requires separate literature.

**3. Collective efficacy causal chain**
The environment → collective efficacy → crime reduction chain is plausible and supported in broader literature, but it is not what this paper demonstrates. The brief should clarify that this causal chain comes from a different body of literature, not from the 17A review.

**4. Integration complexity — future review item**
The increasing interaction among DoDA, CRC, Execution Corps, homelessness teams, and healthcare routing makes the architecture more powerful but also creates implementation risk. Future reviews should begin evaluating operational complexity as well as theoretical coherence.
